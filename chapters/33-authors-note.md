# Sic Semper

## Extra 33: Little Known Facts

Lincoln stood on the parapet at Fort Stevens in July 1864, the only sitting President ever to come under enemy fire in battle.

Booth's first plan was to kidnap Lincoln, not kill him, and trade him for Confederate prisoners. The attempt on March 17, 1865 failed because Lincoln changed his plans that afternoon and never came down the road.

On April 10, the morning after Appomattox, Lincoln asked a crowd at the White House for "Dixie," and told them the Attorney General had ruled it a lawful prize.

On April 11 he spoke from a window about giving the vote to Black men, "the very intelligent, and those who serve our cause as soldiers." Booth was standing below, and is recorded as saying it would be the last speech he ever made.

On April 14 Booth rode a bay mare with a white star from Pumphrey's stable, and was warned about her halter.

That same afternoon Mrs. Surratt drove to her tavern with a package that held field glasses.

Booth left his card at the Kirkwood House for Andrew Johnson.

He handed a letter to the actor John Mathews to give to the newspapers.

The Grants left town that afternoon. Julia Grant remembered a horseman who rode up and stared into their carriage.

The chair of the President's guard was empty, and Booth fired on a laugh line in the third act.

Lewis Powell got into Secretary Seward's house by claiming to bring medicine from his doctor, beat Frederick Seward with a revolver that misfired, and ran into the street shouting that he was mad. Seward and his household all lived.

George Atzerodt took a room above the Vice President's, drank in the bar below, and never went upstairs.

Lincoln was laid slantwise across a bed too short for him in the Petersen house, and died there at twenty-two minutes past seven the next morning.

Thomas Jones hid Booth in the pines for days.

Booth was shot in a burning tobacco barn on the Garrett farm. At the end he looked at his hands and said "Useless, useless."

Louis Weichmann and John Lloyd both testified at the trial. Powell was brought to Mrs. Surratt's door carrying a pickaxe, and she said "Before God, sir, I do not know this man."

President Johnson said he never saw the plea for mercy for Mrs. Surratt. The hangings were on July 7, with one day's notice.

John Surratt gave a public lecture about the plot in Rockville, Maryland, in December 1870.

Lincoln received a great many warning letters. A friend remembered him saying he had eighty of them, and his secretaries wrote that he kept them in an envelope marked *Assassination.*

The Seikilos song is carved on a tombstone found in Turkey in 1883. It is the oldest complete song, words and music, that survives anywhere.
