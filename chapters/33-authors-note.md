# Sic Semper

## Extra 33: Facts

Lincoln stood on the parapet at Fort Stevens in July 1864, the only sitting President ever to come under enemy fire in battle.

Booth's first plan was to kidnap Lincoln, not kill him, and trade him for Confederate prisoners. The attempt on March 17, 1865 failed because Lincoln changed his plans that afternoon and never came down the road.

On April 10, the morning after Appomattox, Lincoln asked a crowd at the White House for "Dixie," and told them the Attorney General had ruled it a lawful prize.

On April 11 he spoke from a window about giving the vote to Black men, "the very intelligent, and on those who serve our cause as soldiers." Booth was standing below, and is recorded as saying it would be the last speech he ever made.

On April 14 Booth rode a bay mare with a white star from Pumphrey's stable, and was warned she would break her halter if left alone.

That same afternoon Mrs. Surratt drove to her tavern with a package from Booth that held field glasses.

Booth left his card at the Kirkwood House for Andrew Johnson: "Don't wish to disturb you. Are you at home?"

He handed a letter to the actor John Mathews to give to the newspapers. Mathews read it and burned it.

The Grants left town that afternoon. On the way to the train, a horseman galloped past their carriage and stared in. Grant later learned it was Booth.

The President's guard, John Parker, was away from his post. Booth timed his shot to a laugh line in the third act.

Lewis Powell got into Secretary Seward's house by claiming to bring medicine from his doctor, beat Frederick Seward with a revolver that misfired, and ran into the street shouting "I'm mad! I'm mad!" All five men he hurt that night lived.

George Atzerodt took a room at the Kirkwood House, where the Vice President was staying, drank in the bar, and never went upstairs.

Lincoln was laid diagonally across a bed too short for him in the Petersen house, and died there at twenty-two minutes past seven the next morning.

Thomas Jones hid Booth in the woods for days and brought him the newspapers.

Booth was shot in a burning tobacco barn on the Garrett farm. At the end he asked to see his hands and said "Useless, useless."

Three nights after the shooting, while detectives were in Mrs. Surratt's boarding house, Powell turned up at her door carrying a pickaxe. She said "Before God, sir, I do not know this man."

Louis Weichmann and John Lloyd both testified against her at the trial. President Johnson said he never saw the plea for mercy for Mrs. Surratt. She was hanged on July 7.

John Surratt gave a public lecture about the plot in Rockville, Maryland, in December 1870.

Lincoln received a great many warning letters. He said he had eighty of them, and kept them in an envelope marked *Assassination.*

The Seikilos song is carved on a tombstone found in Turkey in 1883. It is the oldest complete song, words and music, that survives anywhere.
